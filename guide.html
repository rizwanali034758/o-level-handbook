🎯 The honest version

Stop counting hours.
Start finishing things.

Nobody gets an A* by sitting at a desk longer. They get it by repeating one simple loop until the exam. Here is that loop, and everything around it.

The loop

Six steps. Do them for every single topic.

Learn it

Read the chapter or watch the lesson. Make short notes — not a copy of the book.

Close the book

Write down what you remember. This feels uncomfortable. That discomfort is the learning.

Check

Open your notes. Whatever you forgot is your real weak spot. Nothing else is.

Do questions

Solve topical past-paper questions on that exact topic. Not the whole paper yet.

Mark honestly

Use the official mark scheme. Do not be kind to yourself — the examiner will not be.

Write the mistake down

Keep one notebook called MY MISTAKES. Before exams, revise that instead of the textbook.

Why rereading does not work

This is the single biggest thing most students get wrong.

😴 What most people do

Read → highlight → read again. Your eyes are busy so it feels like work. But you are only learning to recognise the page, not to remember the content.

💪 What actually works

Close everything and try to recall it. Struggling to remember is what builds the memory. If it feels easy, you are probably not learning.

🔁 Then space it out

Revisit a topic the next day, then after a week, then after a month. A topic learnt in September is gone by May unless you go back to it.

Command words: what the question is really asking

The first word tells you what to do. Ignoring it is the cheapest way to lose marks.

State / Give

A short answer. No explanation needed. Writing more earns nothing extra.

Describe

Say what happens or what something is like. No reasons needed.

Explain

Give reasons. Say why or how. This is where most marks live.

Calculate

Show your working and keep the units. Working earns marks even if the answer is wrong.

Suggest

Apply what you know to a new situation you have not seen before.

Evaluate / Discuss

Give both sides, then reach a judgement. One-sided answers cannot get full marks.

Do this today Open your syllabus PDF and find its command-word table. The exact wording changes slightly between subjects, and your syllabus is the version that counts.

A realistic school-day plan

Copy the shape, not the exact times.

🍽️ First — rest

Eat and switch off for a bit. Do not start studying the second you walk in.

1️⃣ 50 min — hardest subject

Your best focus goes to your worst subject. Usually Maths.

☕ 10 min — real break

Walk, water, stretch. Not your phone. A phone break is not a break.

2️⃣ 50 min — content subject

Biology, Pak Studies, Islamiyat — something you learn rather than calculate.

3️⃣ 50 min — questions

Past-paper practice and marking. This block matters most and gets skipped most.

🔚 15 min — close out

Flashcards, formulas, or read your mistakes notebook.

2–3 focused hours is enough on a school day Saying "I will study 10 hours daily" almost always ends after three days. Consistency beats intensity every time.

Your last six months

6 months left

Most of the syllabus covered. Start naming your weak topics honestly.

4 months left

Close the remaining gaps. More past papers. Start mixing topics together.

3 months left

Full papers, timed properly. A clock on the table, no pausing.

2 months left

Past papers become most of your week. Analyse every mistake.

Final month

Timed papers, mistakes notebook, formulas and definitions. Do not start pretty new notes now.

Exam day

📋 Check before you leave

Date, reporting time, venue, which paper, your ID, your Statement of Entry, allowed calculator and equipment.

⏱️ Watch the marks

Do not spend 15 minutes perfecting a 2-mark answer. The marks tell you how much time a question deserves.

➡️ Move on when stuck

Leave it, come back later. Sitting frozen on one question is how people run out of time.

🔍 Save time to check

Units, calculations, skipped questions, and pages you may have turned past without noticing.

One last thing

Do not ask "how many hours does an A* student study?" Ask instead: what can they do that I cannot do yet? Maybe they factorise faster. Maybe they explain electromagnetic induction properly. Maybe they just make fewer careless mistakes. All of those are skills you can train. Turn every weakness into a task with a deadline, and the mystery disappears.
